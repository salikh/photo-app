# 053: Decision: should a reject beat a newer sidecar that says 1 or 2 stars?

- Type: question (answered)
- Blocks: [021](021.md) (the reject rule only)
- Status: done
- Refs: [023](023.md) (survey and the newest-wins decision), [049](049.md) (darktable's default 1 star)

When a Photo's DNG and JPG sidecars disagree, the app shows the newest sidecar's rating (your decision
in 023). The whole-library survey found 87 Photos where one sidecar says **reject (-1)** and the other
something else: in 75 of them the newer sidecar wins and the reject is hidden:

| Sidecars (rated values) | Photos | Newest-wins shows reject | Shows the other value |
|---|---|---|---|
| -1 vs 1 | 82 | 8 | 74 |
| -1 vs 2 | 5 | 4 | 1 |

Example: `2018/2018-01-Yaita/K___0253`: `DNG.xmp` says -1, `JPG.xmp` says 1, the app shows 1.
A "1" that darktable wrote as its default (049) probably does not mean "I changed my mind".

## Options

1. Keep pure newest-wins. Simple and predictable; 75 rejects stay hidden until you rate the Photo
   again (a write syncs both sidecars).
2. A reject beats a newer non-reject when the other value is 0 or 1 (a probable default), otherwise
   newest wins. Shows most of the 74 hidden rejects; a deliberate un-reject to 1 star would be
   overridden until you rate it again.
3. A reject always wins over any other sidecar value until the Photo is rated again. Safest for
   culling (nothing marked "reject" disappears); a real change of mind needs one explicit rating.

Proposed: option 2. Nothing is implemented; the rule is in `photoapp/ratings.py` (`resolve`).

## Note (2026-09-21)

The new requirement in [021](021.md) says to just take the newest rating from the database or any XMP file. That is option 1 here (pure newest-wins); it does not by itself decide whether a reject should be protected, so this ticket stays open until you confirm option 1 or choose 2 or 3.

## Answer

Darktable considers the DNG and JPG as separate images, so some of the ratings are due to the user wanting to leave only one of the pair. This should be handled separately in this app, and the combined image should never be rejected if any of the DNG and JPG is picked. When handling the rejects made in this app, newest should win.

## Interpretation and decision (recorded 2026-09-21)

The answer is none of options 1-3. darktable treats the DNG and the JPG as **separate images**, so a reject on one
sidecar often means "keep only the other file of the pair". The app's Photo is the combined image, so:

1. **A single file's reject never rejects the Photo while the other file is picked.** If any sidecar of the Photo has a
   positive rating (a pick, 1..5 stars), the Photo is not rejected; its rating is that of the newest picked sidecar.
2. **Rejects made in this app: newest wins.** The app writes a reject to both sidecars (and the database), so nothing is
   picked in the pair and the reject stands; when the app's decision is the newest, it wins over older values.
3. Otherwise (no picked sidecar) the normal newest-wins rule of [021](021.md) applies.

Effect on the whole-library survey: the 74 (-1 vs 1) and 1 (-1 vs 2) Photos already shown as 1 or 2 stay that way (now
by rule, not by chance), and the 8 + 4 that were shown as rejects because the reject sidecar was the newest become 1
and 2 stars. So the hidden-reject worry from this ticket goes away: those rejects were per-file, not Photo rejects.

Assumption to veto (not covered by the answer): a reject against an *unrated* (0) sidecar has nothing to protect, so
the normal newest-wins rule applies to that pair.
