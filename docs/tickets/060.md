# 060: Pinch zoom in and out of the zoomed (original size) photo on tablets

- Type: task
- Status: open
- Blocked by: [062](062.md) (only the real-device check; the rest can proceed)
- Priority: later (user request 2026-09-21, "for later")
- Related: [027](027.md) Loupe view, [045](045.md) Swipe navigation, [044](044.md) Phone layout, [050](050.md) Preloading
- Refs: `photoapp/static/loupe.js` (`toggleZoom`, `zoomed`), `photoapp/static/gestures.js`, `photoapp/static/style.css` (`.stage.zoomed`)
- Devices: mostly iPad (Safari), best if it also works on Android tablets (Chrome) and touch laptops

In the zoomed state (the photo at its original size) let the user **zoom in and out with the usual gestures**
(pinch), and pan by dragging, on tablets.

## Today

- Zoom is a two-state toggle: `Z` or a tap swaps the `Medium` image for the full-size `Huge` one and shows
  it at 100% inside a scrollable box (`.stage.zoomed`, native scrolling, `touch-action: pan-x pan-y
  pinch-zoom`).
- There is no in-between zoom. On a touch device the browser's own pinch zoom would scale the **whole page**
  (including the buttons and header), not the photo, so it does not do what a photo viewer should.
- Swipes for navigation and rating are switched off while zoomed ([045](045.md)).
- The viewport meta does not disable page zoom (kept for accessibility).

## Target

- **Pinch** with two fingers zooms the photo in and out around the point between the fingers; **one-finger
  drag** pans; a **double tap** toggles between fit-to-screen and 100% (zooming to the tapped point). The
  buttons and the header never scale.
- Zoom range: from "fit to screen" up to a maximum (proposal: 400% of the original pixels, or at least what
  is sharp); the picture cannot be dragged out of view (clamped, with a soft edge).
- Smooth on an iPad: transform-based (`translate` + `scale` with `will-change`), no layout on every move,
  inertia on release, ~60 fps.
- While loading the full-size image the scaled `Medium` one is shown, then swapped without a jump.
- Leaving the zoom (double tap to fit, `Z`, Esc) or moving to another photo resets it; swiping to the next
  photo works again at fit size. At the edge of a zoomed photo a further drag can still be a swipe to the
  next photo (decide; default: no, only at fit size).
- Also on desktop for free: `Ctrl`+wheel and trackpad pinch (they arrive as `ctrl+wheel`) and `+` / `-`
  keys zoom the same way; the mouse drag pans.

## Design points

- **Pointer Events with `touch-action: none`** on the stage (works in iOS Safari 13+ and Chrome/Android),
  tracking two pointers for the pinch. iOS also fires proprietary `gesturestart/change/end`; prevent their
  default so the page itself does not zoom. The rest of the UI keeps the browser's normal behavior.
- **Memory limits:** iOS Safari limits decoded image sizes; the photos here are about 16 megapixels
  (4928x3264 is 16.1 MP), close to the limit for some formats. Test full-size zoom on a real iPad, and have a
  fallback (a smaller `Huge`, or tiles) if it fails. Watch memory when preloading `Huge` ([050](050.md)).
- **Interplay with the existing gesture code** (`gestures.js`): the swipe detector must stay off while
  zoomed or pinching, and a pinch must never start a swipe or a rating change.
- **Accessibility:** keep pinch on the rest of the page working, respect reduced-motion for the inertia.
- **Testing:** Chrome's DevTools protocol can send multi-touch events (`Input.dispatchTouchEvent`), so the
  pinch math can be tested in Playwright; the iPad itself needs a real-device check (real-device checks are
  on hold for now).

## Result (2026-09-21)

`static/zoom.js` (new; the math is pure and exported), wired from `loupe.js`, `gestures.js` (a second finger cancels a swipe in
progress), styles in `style.css`.

- **Pinch** with two fingers zooms around the point between them: the point of the photo that was under the midpoint stays under it
  (tested to within 4 px); pinching in past the fitted size leaves the zoom. Range: fit to 4x the original pixels, clamped.
- **One-finger drag** (or the mouse) pans a zoomed photo, clamped so the photo cannot be dragged out of view (tested: exactly at the
  far and near edges); panning never navigates. At fit size a drag is a swipe as before.
- **Double tap** toggles between fit and 100% of the original pixels, around the tapped point (touch); a click toggles with a mouse.
  A single tap on touch no longer zooms (it used to), because a double tap would otherwise cancel itself.
- **Desktop:** `Ctrl`+wheel and a trackpad pinch (they arrive as `Ctrl`+wheel) zoom around the pointer, `+`/`-` zoom around the middle,
  a plain wheel pans a zoomed photo, `Z` toggles as before.
- **Only the photo scales:** the photo is drawn at its original size and moved by a CSS transform; `touch-action: none` on the stage keeps
  the browser from zooming the page (tested: `visualViewport.scale` stays 1, the buttons and the strip do not move, no horizontal page
  scroll). The rest of the page keeps the browser's normal behavior.
- **Smooth image swap:** the photo is shown scaled from the 2000 px picture at once and swapped for the full-size one when it has loaded
  (usually already preloaded by 050), with no jump. Zoom pressed before the picture has loaded waits for it.
- **Navigation, closing and resize:** moving to another photo resets the zoom; swiping stays off while zoomed.
- Not done: inertia after a flick (the ticket text mentioned it; the pan follows the finger exactly and stops with it), and
  the real-device check (below).
- Tests in `tests/e2e/test_ui.py`, with real multi-touch sent through the DevTools protocol: pure math; pinch out anchored at the
  fingers and the page not scaling; pinch back to fit; cap at 4x; drag and clamping; double tap to the tapped point; a pinch at fit
  size does not navigate or rate; navigation resets; `Ctrl`+wheel and `+`/`-`; mouse click and drag; zoom before the picture loads.
  The existing tap-toggle test became a double-tap test.
- **Still open: the check on a real iPad and an Android tablet** (memory limits for 16-megapixel images on iPad Safari, smoothness),
  see question ticket [062](062.md).
